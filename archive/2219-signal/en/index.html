Signal

How it started

The MC ledger keeps its clock by timing pulsars, on every world, all the time. That is why it heard first.

  1. 2219-09-18 03:12:07 UTC · Farside

    A residual nobody expected

    The ledger node on the far side of the Moon times twelve pulsars to keep the MC clock. At 03:12 it logged a narrow tone at 1420.405 MHz, the frequency of cold hydrogen, switching on and off. It was filed as local interference.

  2. 2219-09-18 · within 25 minutes

    Every other ledger heard it too

    Earth, Isidis on Mars and the Ceres node logged the identical pattern, each at a different moment. Ledgers compare notes at light speed, so the comparison alone took most of the day.

  3. 2219-09-18

    The delays point at one star

    The arrival times fit a single wave from one direction outside the solar system: RA 03h 32m 55.8s, Dec −09° 27′ 30″. That is Epsilon Eridani, an orange star a little smaller and younger than the Sun, 10.47 light years away.

  4. 2219-09-21

    It is not natural

    The pulses are separated by 2, 3, 5, 7, 11 … 47 units: the prime numbers, in order. No pulsar, maser or reflection does that. Then 667 bits follow, and the whole sequence repeats.

  5. 2219-09-24

    The transmitter is on a turning world

    The tone drifts up and down by a few hertz every 31.6 hours. The transmitter stands on a planet that turns once in 31.6 hours, and whoever built it did not correct for that.

  6. 2219-09-27

    The 667 bits are a picture

    667 is 23 × 29. Folded into rows of 23, the bits make a drawing. Fold it yourself.

  7. 2219-10-05

    Made public

    Every ledger published its raw recordings on the same day. This page is part of that announcement.

Decode

Fold the beacon

You have the same data the ledgers had. Three steps from noise to a picture.

1 · Count the gaps

The preamble is a row of pulses with prime-numbered gaps. Its only job is to say "this is deliberate".

2 · Read the payload

Then 667 bits, ones and zeros, and nothing to say how they fit together.

3 · Fold it

Lay the bits out in rows. Try different widths; only one makes sense.
