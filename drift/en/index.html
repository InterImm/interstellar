    How this is modelled

    Ross 128's measured position, parallax and proper motion (Gaia DR3) and radial velocity (−31.0 km/s, Pulkovo compilation) give its velocity relative to the Sun: 37.9 km/s in total, 31.0 of it straight toward us. The model moves it in a straight line at that velocity and measures everything from the Sun. Gravity from the Galaxy and from passing stars is left out, which is fine for tens of thousands of years and drifts by a few tenths of a light year over a hundred thousand.

    The Earth transit zone is the band within 0.266° of the plane of Earth's orbit, the Sun's radius seen from 1 au. A world inside it sees Earth cross the Sun once a year. The model holds that plane fixed at its J2000 orientation, as Kaltenegger & Faherty (Nature, 2021) did; they found Ross 128 inside the zone from about 1040 BCE to 1120 CE, and this model gives 1142 BCE to 1119 CE. Letting the plane wobble as it really does moves the start to about 1390 BCE and leaves the end where it is.

    Closest approach: this model gives 6.34 ly about 71,000 years from now. García-Sánchez et al. (2001), with older data and a galactic potential, found 6.23 ly at about the same time. Brightness uses absolute magnitudes 4.83 for the Sun and 13.53 for Ross 128 and ignores dust. Story time is real time plus 70,491 days; the stars move on real time, so "now" here is the story's present, 2219.
